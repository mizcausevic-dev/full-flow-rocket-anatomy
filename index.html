One engine. Every drop does two jobs before it burns.

In a full-flow staged combustion engine, all of the fuel and all of the oxidizer pass through a preburner and a turbine before reaching the main chamber. Nothing is thrown overboard to drive the pumps. This explainer traces that loop in five acts, from feed lines to nozzle expansion, without inventing a single pressure, RPM, or angle.

How to read this diagram

  • Cyan is the primary propellant pathway. Follow it and you follow mass through the engine.
  • Gold is actuation and energy: valves, turbine work, hot-gas drive, shaft power (dashed).
  • Violet is sensing and state change: preburner state, sensor points (S). Violet marks where the story changes, not a physical color.
  • Structure stays muted #344257 so hardware, fields, and math never compete.

What “full-flow” actually means

Both propellants, in full, go through a preburner first. The fuel-rich preburner drives the fuel turbine. The oxidizer-rich preburner drives the oxidizer turbine. Then both hot-gas streams go to the main chamber and burn there. No turbine drive gas is dumped overboard. That is the whole claim, and it is qualitative by design in this piece.

Not shown as fact: any pressure, temperature, RPM, thrust, gimbal angle, or pump arrangement beyond “separate shafts.” If a mechanism could not be drawn faithfully, it is drawn as a labeled schematic.
